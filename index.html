FAQ

Why does the App Store say ProxyDeck instead of FastNet?

They are two apps that work together: ProxyDeck — Network Lab is the free iPhone app, and FastNet Connect is the $29 app for your Mac or Windows PC. ProxyDeck also includes standalone network diagnostics that need no computer or purchase. Its Mac Bridge mode, which serves Windows PCs as well, connects to FastNet Connect only while ProxyDeck remains in the foreground.

How does this work?

Carriers track hotspot usage separately from phone data. FastNet Connect hands your computer's traffic to ProxyDeck on the iPhone, and ProxyDeck opens the connections itself, the way any app on the phone does. To the carrier, it looks like regular phone data, so it uses your unlimited phone data instead of your limited hotspot allowance. A Mac reaches the phone over USB or nearby peer-to-peer Wi-Fi; a Windows PC reaches it over the iPhone's Wi-Fi network.

How is this different from PDANet+?

PDANet+ pioneered this approach years ago and still works for some users. The differences: FastNet Connect is a native app for current macOS and for Windows 10 and 11, ProxyDeck is built for modern iOS, and the setup requires no terminal or manual proxy configuration. If PDANet+ already works for you, no need to switch. Where PdaNet+ stands on iPhone today.

Is FastNet a PairVPN alternative?

Yes, for the specific job of getting a Mac or a Windows PC online through a nearby iPhone. PairVPN's iOS app was removed from the App Store in October 2026 and its Android build has not been updated since 2023. FastNet Connect pairs with ProxyDeck, with a $29 one-time license and a foreground-only iPhone app. It does not replace PairVPN's remote home-network access or its Android client. What happened to PairVPN, and what changes on a Mac, and the same for a Windows PC.

Why pay $29 if I can do this with iproxy + a SOCKS5 server?

You can. iproxy 1082 1082 plus a SOCKS5 server on iPhone is the same idea, free, and well-documented. FastNet is for people who want it to just work without terminal commands, manual port forwarding, or remembering to set the system proxy each time. If you're comfortable in terminal and only need it occasionally, the manual setup is fine.

Will this be detected by my carrier?

Tethering detection usually relies on TTL inspection, User-Agent fingerprinting, and DNS heuristics. Because ProxyDeck's Mac Bridge uses the phone's TCP stack, traffic originates from the same network stack as other iPhone apps. No tool guarantees invisibility, and carriers may still infer usage from traffic patterns.

What speeds can I expect?

Usually whatever your cellular signal delivers. On a Mac over USB, Lightning-port iPhones cap around 280–320 Mbps because Lightning is USB 2.0, and USB-C iPhones (15 Pro and later) can push gigabit if the signal supports it. Over Wi-Fi, which is how a Windows PC connects, the wireless link between the two devices also plays a part. Real-world speeds depend on tower congestion and signal strength.

Is this legal?

FastNet is a standard SOCKS5 proxy — the same technology used by millions of developers daily. How you use your cellular data is between you and your carrier. We recommend reviewing your carrier's terms of service.

On a Mac: USB or Wi-Fi?

Either works. USB is a direct cable link between your Mac and iPhone: most reliable, lowest latency, and the phone charges while connected. Wi-Fi is a direct device-to-device link (like AirDrop) — no cable, and you don't need to be on any network, just have Wi-Fi on. Either way your Mac's traffic leaves the phone as ordinary phone data, not tethered hotspot data.

How does a Windows PC connect?

Over Wi-Fi. The PC joins the iPhone's Wi-Fi network, which away from home is its Personal Hotspot network, and pairs with ProxyDeck by scanning a code once. That network is only the local link between the two devices: the PC's internet traffic is handed to ProxyDeck, which sends it out as the phone's own. Download and setup for Windows.

Does it work with my carrier, and outside the US?

FastNet does not depend on a particular carrier or country. Your computer's traffic leaves the iPhone as ordinary phone data on whatever SIM is in it, which covers T-Mobile, Verizon, AT&T, Mint Mobile, Visible, and Cricket in the US as well as carriers elsewhere. It helps when your plan gives the hotspot less data or speed than the phone itself. If it does not work on your carrier, the 30-day refund applies.
